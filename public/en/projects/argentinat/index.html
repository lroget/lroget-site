What it is

A photo, an identification, a data point.

iNaturalist is a citizen science platform that lets anyone upload a photo of an organism and identify it. The community then validates that identification. ArgentiNat is the node for Argentina, with its own community and projects. It is one of the leading citizen science platforms in the country. It is also the largest collection of photos of Argentine biodiversity, most of them under open licenses.

I launched and led it. It was developed within Fundación Vida Silvestre Argentina, with a grant from the National Geographic Society.

Timeline

012018–2021

From Mexico to launch

In 2018 I was invited to the first Latin American workshop on citizen science, in Mexico City (24 and 25 September), organized by National Geographic, iNaturalist and CONABIO. I was the Argentine user with the second-most observations on iNaturalist, a platform very little used in the country at that point. At that workshop I saw how important citizen science is for environmental education and conservation. From that opportunity, I proposed the idea to Vida Silvestre.

I presented the proposal at Vida Silvestre. ArgentiNat was developed with a National Geographic Society grant, awarded to me in 2019; it was carried out throughout that year to reach the launch. I wrote the proposal and the reports. On 31 October 2019 I attended the Second Latin American Meeting of the iNaturalist network, in Mérida, Yucatán.

The platform was presented on 21 November 2019 at the Museo Argentino de Ciencias Naturales, where I was the keynote speaker. There was also a panel that I moderated with participants from GBIF, Aves Argentinas and Naturalista.mx. The name ArgentiNat was first mentioned on 14 November, the day Argentina reached 100,000 observations on iNaturalist.

An important part of the work in 2019 was tackling the translation of the site and finding curators to build a group of volunteers: experts in areas such as plants or insects, or simply reference users.

In 2020, during the pandemic, the platform began to grow.

In January 2021 I wrote the script for three ArgentiNat videos for Vida Silvestre, produced by Tamanduá Estudio: the presentation of the platform, how to make your first observation and how to create a project (in Spanish).

022022–2026

Vida Silvestre Safaris

We reimagined Vida Silvestre's naturalist Safaris through citizen science. They are events in which, for four days each season, the community is invited to record observations on the platform from wherever they are in the country. This way we learn, season after season, the biodiversity characteristic of each one, which matters in a context of climate change and ecological crisis. The most important point is that everyone can take part, whatever their level of knowledge.

The idea is a spiritual successor to the camps of the same name that Vida Silvestre ran in the 1980s and 1990s. We discussed it with Raúl Chiesa, who coordinated the Safaris in that original incarnation, on a panel at Yurumí, Vida Silvestre's annual event, in 2022. They are also direct descendants of the COA's seasonal censuses: the experience with those censuses is what made it possible to create them.

The first edition was the 2022 Winter Safari, from 29 July to 1 August. I coordinated the 2024 editions (autumn, winter and spring), the summer 2025 one and the winter 2026 one. Before that, I co-organized similar events: the City Nature Challenge and the Great Southern Bioblitz.

We also ran special Safaris for companies and foundations: Prosegur, Salesforce and Campo de Mayo, with Fundación Parques.

What was done with the data

Two recent cases where ArgentiNat appears as a source. Neither is my work: it is what others did with the platform.

  • April 2026 Plants of CórdobaA study published in the journal Flora, by researchers from CONICET and the Universidad Nacional de Córdoba, reviewed more than 25,000 observations from about 1,300 people, made on iNaturalist through ArgentiNat, and identified 99 plant species with no previous record in the province. Cadena 3 report (in Spanish).
  • October 2026 Spiders of Urugua-íMonitoring in the Urugua-í Vida Silvestre Reserve (Misiones), with MACN-CONICET researchers and ArgentiNat curators, recorded four spider species with no published scientific records for Argentina. They are not new to science: they are new records for the country. Tiempo Argentino report (in Spanish).

The data come from press reports. I did not read the full scientific papers.

What makes it different

One platform for people who are just starting to look and for people who need data: schools, municipalities, protected areas and research teams.

To get started

Use ArgentiNat

This is all it takes to join in.

  1. An accountCreate an iNaturalist account, on the web or in the app. It is free.
  2. A photoPhotograph what you see: a plant, an insect, a fungus, a bird. Upload it with the place and the date.
  3. A communityOther people identify it, and the observation joins the ArgentiNat project.
  4. A SafariWhen there is a Vida Silvestre Safari, it is a good time to start.
